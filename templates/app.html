Liturgical Calendar for iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and Apple TV

The Lutheran church year, its readings and its propers, in an app that works without a connection.

The same calendar this site has kept since 1997 runs as a native app. It shows the Sunday or festival for any date from 1583 to 2300, its liturgical color and season, and its readings, in both the Lutheran Service Book three-year series and the historic one-year series.

Everything is computed on the device, so the app works on a plane, in a sacristy without signal, or at a desk years from now. It collects nothing and has no account.

Liturgical Calendar Explorer is free on the App Store for iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and Apple TV.

A short tour of the app on every screen (80 seconds)

On iPhone

Five tabs run along the bottom: Today, Calendar, Wheel, Daily and More. The day card gives the name, color, season and church year, how far the church year has come, the appointed readings, the Hymn of the Day, and the LSB Daily Lectionary. In the one-year series it adds the historic introit, collect and gradual. On a weekday it also shows the coming Sunday or festival. Tap a reading to preview its text. Each weekday of Lent has its own card, with the historic propers.

The calendar shows the church year as a list or a month at a time, each day in its color. Pinch the Liturgical Year Wheel to enlarge it, and tap a Sunday or festival to open its day. The introit, collect, gradual and Scripture text can be set larger, up to a Lectern size for reading aloud. In Settings you can choose an app icon for each season of the church year, or let the icon follow the season.

On iPhone Duo

Closed, the outer screen shows the app as on any iPhone. Open it, and the large inner screen shows the full layout, with the sidebar beside the day, the calendar or the wheel, and the readings two by two. Fold or unfold it as you go and the app keeps your place.

On iPad and Mac

The larger screens show the calendar as a full table or a month at a time, the one-year propers for every Sunday and festival, the Daily Lectionary chart, Historic Lent, and the Almanac: moon phases, the date of Easter in the West and the East, and the Paschal full moon back to the Exodus. The wheel enlarges with a pinch or the scroll wheel, and a click on a Sunday or festival opens its day.

On Apple Watch

The watch shows today's Sunday or festival in its liturgical color, with its readings, the Hymn of the Day and the Daily Lectionary. Tap a reading to read the passage. Coming Up lists the next Sundays and festivals, and the church-year wheel shows how far the year has come.

Complications put the day on the watch face. The round one shows the Sunday's short name, such as ADV 1 or PALM SUN, inside a ring of the church year's colors; another marks your place in the church year. The wider ones give the date, the day's name and its Gospel.

On Apple TV

Display mode is made for a narthex or a parish hall. It fills the screen with the day in its color, then its readings and the coming Sundays, one after another, and turns over at midnight.

The Today screen gives the readings and the Hymn of the Day in large print, and a reading opens its full text. The calendar, Historic Lent and Go to Date are on the top menu. The wheel can be enlarged and moved with the remote. The Top Shelf shows today and the coming Sundays.

A widget for the day

Put today's Sunday or festival on the Home Screen, the Lock Screen or the Mac desktop, in its liturgical color. The larger sizes add the readings, the Hymn of the Day and the Daily Lectionary. On the iPhone Lock Screen a round widget shows the Sunday's short name in a ring of the church year's colors, and in StandBy the small widget fills with the day's color. Widgets turn over at midnight.

Print and share

PDF sheets for a day (card or plain), the church-year calendar, the one-year propers, the Daily Lectionary and each Lenten weekday, the same sheets this site makes. Share as Image makes a picture of any day in its color, with its readings and Hymn of the Day, for a message, a post or a bulletin. The wheel as a PNG or SVG poster. An iCal file for any year, or a calendar subscription.

Settings and a Sunday reminder

Choose the lectionary, violet or blue Advent, and the Bible translation once. Your other Apple devices and the widgets follow the same settings through your own iCloud. On iPhone, iPad and Mac you can turn on a reminder the evening before each Sunday and festival, with its readings and Hymn of the Day, at a time you choose.

For planners and scripts

Shortcuts actions on iPhone, iPad and Mac. On the Mac, AppleScript commands and an optional local MCP server, so an AI assistant on your own computer can look up the church year and make the PDFs, calendars and wheels.

Private by design

No account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The app collects nothing. Settings stay in your own iCloud, and reminders are scheduled on the device. The app goes online only to show a Scripture passage you ask to preview, and nothing is stored.

The app follows this site exactly: its readings, propers and calendars are checked against the website for every year it covers. For sources and permissions, see About; for privacy, see Privacy.
